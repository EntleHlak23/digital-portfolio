Why UbuntuConnect exists

Sites like Airbnb and Property24 optimise for the landlord. We optimise for the neighbourhood.

Rising demand from remote workers earning foreign income has pushed short-term rentals ahead of long-term housing in the city centre and surrounds, pricing out long-standing residents. UbuntuConnect separates those two markets, so they stop competing for the same homes.

The problem, in short

Gentrification is a pricing problem before it's anything else

Landlords in the city centre and surrounding neighbourhoods can earn more, faster, from short-term nomad and tourist stays than from a local tenant paying in Rand. That single incentive pushes housing out of reach for working-class residents in areas like Khayelitsha, Mitchells Plain and Observatory, and drives the overcrowding and displacement that follow.

UbuntuConnect doesn't ban short-term stays — it makes them accountable to the community they sit inside.

About the platform

The Ethical Housing Guidelines

Every landlord agrees to these before a listing is approved. They're short on purpose — easy to understand, easy to enforce.

  • 1
    Fair pricing. Long-term rent reflects the local market rate for the area, not what a foreign-currency income could bear.
  • 2
    Category commitment. A property lists as long-term or short-term, and switching requires re-approval — no quiet conversion once a resident has moved in.
  • 3
    Community contribution. Short-term bookings include a small fee that funds housing initiatives in the same area.
  • 4
    Transparency. Every listing shows its category, price history, and whether it has ever changed category.
